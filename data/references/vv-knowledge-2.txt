- For an expanded and more advanced version of the below, click through to read the “Introduction to COOs” article written by Wolff.
- Vintage in the SW context relates to items from the original era of 1977 to the end of the 80s.
- This timeline encapsulates Kenner preproduction through to the Brazilian Glasslite and bootleg figures produced in the late 80s.
- “Modern” in the context of Vintage SW collecting refers to the 90s onwards.
- This includes the POTF2 line, Bend-Ems and everything beyond that.
- However, there's a linguistic glitch on the horizon with these early 90s pieces, some of which are now more than 20 years old - the definitive threshold -
- “Kenner”, “Kenner Products”, “General Mills Fun Group” or “GM”
- Founded in 1946 and based in Cincinnati, Ohio, Kenner is the brand who acquired the licence to produce Star Wars action figures in 1976.
- During the SW era, the Kenner company was owned by General Mills (acquired in 1967).
- Variants
- MOC (Mint on card)
- A popular hobby acronym used to abbreviate what is essentially a carded figure with the bubble still sealed and intact.

- Copyright & date stamp
- GMFGI is an abbreviation of “General Mills Fun Group Inc.”
- LFL is an abbreviation of “Lucas Film Limited”.
- Country of Origin (COO)
- NO COO (No Country of Origin)
- “Smooth” No COO
- A smooth surface remaining where the letters of the previous COO existed.
- “Blobbed” No COO
- A “blobbed” effect created by loosely drilling over the letters of the previous COO.
- Raised Bar
- Double Raised bar
- “Scarred” No COO
- “Mould”, “mould”, “Mould”, “Tool”
- These are all terms to describe the metal block which is used with an injection moulding machine to form plastic parts.
