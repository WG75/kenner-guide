- This guide is dedicated to the earliest produced figures in Spain, called “Poch” variants.
- The Spanish company Poch (or Novedades Poch) never produced SW action figures on its own.
- In July 1980, three companies including Poch merged to form the PBP company, these companies were: Poch, Borras and Palouzie.
- “Poch” period
- 31 and 37/41a backs
- “Poch” period: 31 and 37/41a backs
- “PBP” period
- 37/41 b backs and later
- “PBP” period: 37/41 b backs and later
- PBP used a totally different material compared to their former production and to the rest of the world. Those figures are unique worldwide.

- There are several characteristics which can be seen on most of the Poch figures.
- These characteristics can of course also appear on other figures which are not produced in Spain.
- It's important for me to stress that not every figure with dents, melt-marks, “dripped” paint or faded/discolored hands is assumed to be a POCH variant.
- As you can see in the pictures above all “Poch” produced pale hands are noticeably similar in appearance.
- "DRIPPED“ PAINT LOOK
- A. It only appears on the limbs (“soft” plastic), never on a torso (“hard” plastic).
- B. It was only found in black or white/pale sprayed areas.
- C. The thickness of the paint seems to affect this phenomenon. It never occurs in thinly sprayed areas.
- After studying these marks for a while I have found out that you can find mainly three different types of melt marks

- The dilemma with the “POCH” variants is, that there is lots of different versions on each of the characters floating around.
- This assumption is difficult to proof for sure, but I will give an example on a more known figure: The Rebel Soldier.
- This Poch variant is one of the most earliest known ones.
- Then I came across a figure that had the torso from a Poch version, but different, moulded legs and different COO.
- First up is a picture of the four POCH Rebel Soldiers
- From left to right
- POCH version with bright white paint to trousers and scarf and crotch (discoloured greenish legs)
- POCH version with bright white paint to trousers and scarf and crotch (slightly discoloured greenish legs)
- POCH version beige moulded trousers, but bright white painted scarf and crotch
- Second is four MIHK Rebel Soldiers with normal (beige) painted pants
- Kenner version with beige paint to trousers and scarf and crotch, pinkish face
- Kenner version with beige paint to trousers and scarf and crotch, tan face
- Kenner version with beige paint to trousers and scarf and crotch, sth. between tan and pinkish face
- Kenner version with beige paint to trousers and scarf and crotch, pale face (not discoloured!)
- Third for comparison is the 2-line HK. This figure is assumed to be Asian overstock that got mixed-up in the Spanish factories
- Kenner version with beige moulded trousers and beige painted scarf and crotch, light brown paint and “big” eyes.
- I have called the “1-line large HK” the “A” mould and the “MIHK” the “B” mould
